# Scale Pai Sho — Rules

English rules for the current game: local influence, cumulative scores for both players, and immediate victory by capturing four identical opposing tiles.

1. **Equipment.** Two players, Player 1 followed by Player 2, play without randomness on a circular board of 113 intersections. An intersection is a crossing of a horizontal and vertical grid line; it holds at most one tile. Each player has twelve tiles: four 1s, four 2s and four 3s. Tiles 1 and 2 are flowers; tile 3 is a blue boat with no special powers beyond its number. The board starts empty unless the optional setup in rule 18 is used. Players never swap sides.

2. **A turn.** You may place at most one reserve tile and use your available moves, in either order. Placing does not use a move; moving does not grant another placement. You must place or move at least one tile: passing is forbidden. You may move a tile you just placed, and move the same tile more than once.

3. **Move allowance.** A normal turn allows two moves. You may save **one unused move**, until used, or borrow a move from your next turn, which then allows only one move and no further borrowing. Never make more than three moves in a turn. Two economical turns cannot save two moves. Placing without moving can also save a move, subject to the same cap. The balance starts at zero: +1 means a saved move, −1 a borrowed move to repay. At the end of a turn, the new balance is min(1, previous balance + 2 − moves made). Placements do not enter this calculation. The last personal turn always allows at most one move.

4. **Movement.** Each move travels exactly the tile's number in a straight horizontal, vertical or diagonal line. Each diagonal step counts as one space. The destination and all intermediate intersections must be on the board and empty. You cannot jump over another tile, change direction or stop early. Immobilised tiles cannot move.

5. **Valid triangle.** Three non-collinear tiles belonging to the same player, one of each number, can form a region. It must cover **one to five intersections**, including its interior and edges, whether empty or occupied, excluding its three vertices. Zero or six or more covered intersections makes it invalid.

6. **Declaration.** A potential triangle has no effect until its owner declares it. The owner may always decline, even if only one option exists. A newly declared triangle must become active after its effects resolve: it cannot be created suspended. A formation whose effects cannot stabilise is not a legal declaration.

7. **Formation limits.** A player may have at most four active triangles. A tile in an active triangle cannot belong to another triangle. Tiles of suspended triangles may be reused by replacement under rule 11. There is no limit to the number of successive declarations in a turn.

8. **Active region.** The opponent cannot place or end a move on its covered intersections. However, a 2 or 3 tile may cross those intersections if it lands beyond them, outside active opposing regions, and its path is empty of tiles. Opposing tiles already inside are immobilised, not removed. Your own tiles may move through your regions normally. Opposing regions may overlap.

9. **Crossing a surface.** Crossing lines or surfaces alone blocks nothing. Even a 1 tile can cross a region's surface between two permitted intersections; its range simply does not allow it to jump over an intersection.

10. **Suspension.** If an active region immobilises a vertex of an opposing declared triangle, that triangle is suspended: it stops blocking intersections and releases its captives, unless another active region holds them. It reactivates once no active opposing region holds any of its vertices. Tiles that moved away during suspension do not return. Mere overlap without a captured vertex does not suspend a triangle. For example, B suspends A, then C suspends B, allowing A to reactivate.

11. **Disappearance and replacement.** Moving a vertex removes the triangle in its old arrangement. Tiles of a suspended triangle can form a new valid triangle. This optional replacement removes the old triangle entirely: it can never reactivate. If several suspended triangles are reused, each is entirely removed. A tile can change triangles but never belong to two declared triangles at once. Tiles of an active triangle cannot be reused this way.

12. **Declining.** A declined triangle cannot be offered repeatedly in the same arrangement unless a potential vertex is immobilised and then freed. Moving a vertex also allows a new choice.

13. **Reactions.** After every placement, move or triangle decision, resolve all formation effects before continuing physical actions. Each owner makes their own decisions, including during the opponent's turn, without spending a placement, move or turn. Choices are offered to the author of the physical action first, then the other player. The main player then resumes their remaining actions.

14. **Loops.** The player who initiates a loop of suspensions, reactivations or reformations **loses immediately**, regardless of score. This also applies to reciprocal suspension loops. Mere overlap or a chain that stabilises is not a loop. Surviving triangles retain their previous activity while effects propagate simultaneously. A reciprocal dependency that is already stable does not create a loop. If automatic propagation repeats a state, its triggering action identifies the initiator. If a sequence of decisions repeats a configuration, the initiator is the author of the first decision in the repeated segment, not necessarily the last decision. The configuration includes declarations, declined triangles, activity and the next decision-maker.

15. **Victory by capture.** Simultaneously holding **all four opposing tiles of the same number** wins immediately, regardless of score or whose turn it is. Capture means immobilisation by an active region; no tile is removed. Captives may be spread across several regions, but each tile counts once. Reserve tiles, threatened tiles, freed tiles and tiles covered only by suspended regions do not count. Past captures are not accumulated. Check victory after suspensions and reactivations stabilise, before any further optional triangle decision or turn ending. Reactivation can cause victory. A suspension loop is still a loss under rule 14. No extra turn score is added for an immediate victory during a turn.

16. **Scoring.** Each tile influences the **eight neighbouring intersections of its 3×3 square**, including diagonals, within the board. On each empty intersection, the side with more neighbouring tiles earns **one point**: 1 against 1 is neutral, 2 against 1 earns one point for the majority, and 2 against 2 is neutral. Influence never extends farther or scores occupied intersections. Every tile has the same influence, even when immobilised; regions neither block nor strengthen it. Each immobilised opposing tile earns **4 points**. Each distinct declared opposing triangle with at least one immobilised vertex earns one additional **4-point bonus**, even if suspended. Thus one captured vertex earns 8 points; two vertices of the same triangle earn 12, two from different triangles earn 16. Count each tile and each triangle bonus once. Undeclared candidates and removed triangles earn no bonus. Triangle area itself scores nothing. These values form the current board score. At the end of every complete turn, after reactions, **both players add their entire board scores to their cumulative totals**, whoever just played. A board of 40–42 adds 40 and 42; then 37–45 produces totals of 77–87. An unchanged board of 40–42 for three turns produces 120–126. Ties still award each player's points. Intermediate actions do not add extra scores. Both totals start at zero, with no starting advantage. Optional setup scores nothing. Historical records retain their historical scoring; any old starting compensation is applied only once.

17. **Ending.** Choose 15, 20, 30, 50 or 70 turns **per player**. On each player's final personal turn, at most one move is allowed; a placement remains independently available. Resolve reactions before scoring. Unused moves and reserve tiles score nothing. Without an immediate victory, the highest cumulative score wins at the turn limit; equal scores draw. **If only one side is fully blocked, the cumulative score decides; if neither can play, the game is a draw regardless of score.** Fully blocked means having no legal placement or move after reactions, judged with an available turn's allowance, not merely having used the current allowance. Triangle choices and ending a turn do not avoid blockage. If blockage ends a turn in which a placement or move was made, close and score that turn once after reactions. An already-scored start of turn is not scored again. Loop losses and four-identical capture victories take priority over score and blockage.

18. **North/south option.** Choose Player 1's hemisphere; Player 2 takes the other. All placements remain restricted to the respective hemisphere throughout the game; the centre line is shared. Moves may cross the dividing line under the usual rules. Before normal turns, Player 1 then Player 2 alternate free placements of zero to four tiles from their twelve-tile stocks. Either may finish setup early; the other may continue to four tiles. Setup and its reactions score no cumulative points and use no normal turns or moves. Once both setups and reactions finish, Player 1 takes the first ordinary turn.
